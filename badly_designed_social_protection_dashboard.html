Teacher's Pedagogical Guide

Deconstructing the 6 Elements of Digital Governance

Use this sidebar in class to demonstrate how **neglecting the 6 core elements** yields a dashboard that is confusing, misleading, and dangerous for public policy decision-making.

1. Lack of Purpose

**The Flaw:** Technical IT management (server requests, latency, raw logs) is mixed with public policy outcome indicators.

👉 **Ask the class:** Does a Minister of Social Development need real-time HTTP server request rates to make policy decisions?

2. Lack of Data Governance

**The Flaw:** Unstandardized data devoid of context. Absolute dollar values, percentages without denominators, and daily vs. monthly rates are dumped on one screen.

👉 **Ask the class:** How can anyone meaningfully compare 42,000 monthly doctor visits against $12M in cloud server costs?

3. Flawed Tools

**The Flaw:** Information overload. High-tech charts are chosen for visual appeal rather than conceptual accuracy, resulting in visual noise.

4. Absence of Process

**The Flaw:** The dashboard is not linked to operational workflows. When an indicator shifts, no clear action protocol or responsible role is designated.

5. Faulty Methodology — The Critical Failure

**The Flaw (False Causality):**

  • CCT vs. Unemployment: Assumes transfers *cause* unemployment, ignoring omitted variables like macroeconomic shocks or informal labor markets.
  • Vaccines vs. School Dropout: Confuses temporal coincidence with causality (both are conditional requirements of the social program, not direct cause-and-effect).
6. Ignoring People (User Experience & Literacy)

**The Flaw:** Designed without user empathy. Decision-makers without advanced statistical training will blindly accept automated system recommendations.
